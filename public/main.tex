\section{The World}\label{_introduction}

\paragraph{History.}
Picture a scenic valley, rich in natural resources and history,
nestled between steep mountains and an unexplored, wild ocean.
Twenty years ago, the False Sylph ravaged its entirety. Everyone who
did not go mad or vanished without a trace fled to the single mountain
pass leading out of the region.
Some towns were purposely burned down, some
were abandoned before the Sylph ever came. Bandits preying upon the
refugees, carrying all their worldly possessions with them. Towns,
mines, forests, and fields lay empty and barren.

In the paranoia and the confusion, people only agreed on one fact: The
Sylph needed people to stay alive. So the pass was magically sealed and
a harbor fortress erected. With the only two known connections
closely guarded, time was left to run its course.

Many left. The new border region, supplied mostly by the rich lands now sealed off, could not support everyone. Some stayed, to wait, to guard the pass, sometimes to find another way back in. No attempt was allowed to succeed.

Stories about the catastrophe and its cause grew and changed over the
years. Night air leaking out from the ground, vengeful elves poisoning
wells to reclaim their lands, or fairy abductions were just a couple
of rumors peddled by commoners and provocateurs alike. But the most
popular theory was that of the False Sylph, a creature unseen
and unheard, feasting upon the sanity of entire towns.

\paragraph{Today,} after what seems like an eternity, the seal unravels.
An uneasy expedition crosses the mountain pass for the first time in twenty years.
After a decade of meticulous planning, they are prepared to face every
conceivable scenario. And yet, after a decade of bickering, they are
equally well prepared to fall apart at the first sign of trouble.

\section{The Characters}\label{_characters}

The following lists and tables should help you to get an idea for the
setting and provide inspiration for your character's background and
motivation.

\paragraph{Tone.} It should quickly become obvious that anyone who dares to enter this
cursed valley is at least a little bit unhinged. Greed, hubris, reckless
curiosity or just plain naïvety are not just good reasons to be
here---they are a requirement! While the risk of death for your
character may be quite high, we want to focus on the bright, maybe even
comedic side of this.
Revel in the insanity of their overambitious minds, let them fly close to the sun, and feel glad about the change of pace when they inevitably fall to their doom.
As deadly as the valley
might be, as big are the throngs of foolhardy adventures pressing into
the bountiful region.

\pagebreak

\paragraph{Open Questions.}
Expect to find exciting and incalculable risks like these:

\begin{itemize}
	\item
	      With no existing power structures, how do the different
	      \textbf{factions} control their range of influence?
	\item
	      Did truly \textbf{everyone} leave before the region was sealed?
	\item
	      Was it right to assume that the \textbf{coast} was too steep and the
	      shoals too capricious to allow anyone to land at the coast before the
	      twenty years were over?
	\item
	      What happens to an \textbf{ecology}, dominated by man and its magic,
	      when left to its own devices?
	\item
	      And last but not least: What remains of the \textbf{Sylph}, if it ever
	      existed at all?
\end{itemize}

Which one of these questions keep your character up at night?
Which spike your interest as a player?

\subsection{Protagonist Backgrounds}\label{_character_backgrounds}
Choose a background or use the list as a basis to come up with your own background.

\paragraph{Convict} (Closure) \\
You tried to get back in, to find your missing
relatives, to retrieve what was yours. After twenty years of unjust
imprisonment, you finally get the chance to make you peace. There might
not be much left, but at least you know exactly where to look for it.

\paragraph{Surgeon} (Ambition, Exploration)\\
Any expedition needs some kind of medical support,
and you are the best of your class. Relying on a priest in a godforsaken
land is almost as foolish as believing in the Sylph. You are a man of
science, and you intend to lead the settlers into the Age of
Enlightenment. Any hysteria has a natural, underlying reason, and you
are bent on finding out what actually happened. Maybe this power can be
used for good?

\textbf{Variant}: You were never the religious type, but after you
failed your medical exam twice, you took up a trick or two from an
occult manual. And you found out that you were quite good at it. The
cognitive dissonance between your words and your actions irks you to
this day, and you are looking for a way out. Oh, and there is also the
small problem of repaying your blood debt to your Patron. Any
opportunity to make your fortune, settle down, and stop practicing
``medicine'' would be very welcome indeed.

\paragraph{Archeologist}
(Exploration)\\
Untouched ruins are hard to find nowadays, but an
untouched region is truly unheard of! Twenty years might not be
considered ``archeological'' by most, but funding is impossible to get
anyway. And when you heard reports about what in particular was left
behind, the choice to leave your struggling university department was
not a hard one. Maybe you will finally find enough, both in historical
significance and monetary value, to get tenure?

\paragraph{Noble}
(Restoration)\\
You somehow escaped with a smattering of your
fortune, but your title, your lands, and your mines were left behind. With
your funds drawing low after twenty years in exile, you are adamant to take
what is rightfully yours.

\textbf{Variant}: Your father only left you a deed and some old
documents, but you promised him on his deathbed that you would restore
your family to its former glory.


\paragraph{Messiah} (Religion) \\
Not everyone has been as … accepting of your new
religion as you originally hoped. You never expected a huge following as,
your messianic powers are still developing. But you certainly did not
expect to attract this much attention. Maybe this is the right time for
a promised land?


\paragraph{Bandit} (Treasure)\\
Riches beyond your wildest dreams, stacked high on
the backs of mules and fleeing people alike. You chose to help your
fellow men and lift their burden. Most did not make it anyways.
% "cannot help but" is ideomatic
Still, you cannot help but wonder: Which treasures were left behind?

\textbf{Variant}: You were the last one to leave town. Multiple times!
Who would consider taking abandoned property to be stealing? You escaped
within an inch of your life, but the sight of untold riches, too much to
carry it all, stayed with you. It is time to return.

\paragraph{A New Start} (A Simple Plan)\\
You barely made it to the pass in time for the
first expedition. Truly a once in a lifetime opportunity: To leave your old life behind, to start anew, but without the risk of high seas and unexplored lands.
The bed is already made for you, now you only have to find it…

\paragraph{Bearer of the Torch} (Zealot)\\
You studied the Sylph. You knew how to beat it.
You torched the granaries when everyone left, and you knew that this was
enough. \emph{Twenty years!} But \emph{one} would have sufficed. Over
the din of ignorance and terror, the voice of reason could not be heard.
But this is all in the past. You have sworn an oath, and now only one
task remains: Cull Her children, torch Her saplings, end Her bloodline.
Once and for all.

\textbf{Variant}: After twenty years of mourning, after twenty years of
injustice, after twenty years in hiding, the stars align once again.
There will be no mistakes this time. The Queen is dead, but the princess
survived. And she needs your guidance…

\paragraph{Merchant}
(Naïve Greed)\\
In every tragedy, there is opportunity. A business
opportunity, to be precise. An opportunity no one in their right mind
would ever pass up. Expeditions need gear, settlers need farming
equipment, people need food. Why am I the only one that realizes this?

\section{The Expedition}

As a group, \textit{decide how you entered the valley} by consulting the table on the next page.
Eventually, you will reach Briginia, the closest of the three remaining cities.
But right now, you are passing through the foothills into the floodplains, tracing faded roads, passing overgrown farms, keeping to the creeks that flow out of the mountains.
Hills and erratics dot the landscape, with forests to the north and a river west.

Old maps in hand, your vanguard scouts ahead, but sight lines have changed and landmarks crumbled, making navigation difficult the farther you venture into the heartlands.

Decide on the chief goal of your expedition by \textit{choosing the point of interest} you are exploring first. What do you hope to find?
\begin{itemize}
	\item Are you building an \textbf{outpost} or do you restore existing structures?
	\item Do you trust the land and forage for \textbf{food} and water?
	\item What do you \textbf{import} and where do you acquire the funds to do so?
	\item How important are tools, metal, and \textbf{supplies} to you?
\end{itemize}
